            What plays on a TV

            TV web browsers are much pickier than a Mac. If you can choose, save videos as MP4.

            File typeOn the TV
            MP4, M4V videoPlays on TVThe safe choice, as long as the video inside is H.264 with AAC sound (most are).
            WebM, MOV videoMay not playWorks on some TVs and not others. Try it.
            MKV, AVI, WMV, FLV, MPG, MPEG, TS, 3GP videoNeeds convertingTV browsers can’t open these. HomeFlix can make an MP4 copy if ffmpeg is installed.
            JPG, PNG, GIF, WebP photoShows on TV
            HEIC, HEIF, TIFF, BMP photoShows on TVHomeFlix turns these into JPEG for the TV. Your originals are not changed.

            Other kinds of file, such as documents and music, are left out.

            What to expect from a TV browser

            • MP4 with H.264 video and AAC sound is the safe choice. It plays on practically every Smart TV.
            • MKV and AVI files, HEVC (H.265) video, and AC3 or DTS sound often won’t play, or play with no sound. Convert them to MP4 first.
            • 4K and very detailed videos may stutter over Wi-Fi. A 1080p copy usually plays smoothly. Moving the Mac or TV closer to the router helps too.
            • Some TVs won’t start a video until you press OK or Play, and some dim the screen or go to sleep during long videos. Check the TV’s screen saver or eco settings.
            • This Mac has to stay awake, with HomeFlix open, on the same Wi-Fi. If the lid closes or the Mac sleeps, the TV loses the picture. Keep it plugged in while you watch.
            • Guest Wi-Fi usually won’t work. Guest and “isolated” networks stop devices from talking to each other, so the TV can’t reach the Mac. Use your normal home Wi-Fi for both.

            The TV can’t open the address

            1. Is HomeFlix switched on? The switch at the top of this page should say “On air”.
            2. Same Wi-Fi on both. The TV and this Mac must be on the same home network. A TV on a cable to the same router is fine.
            3. Type the whole address, including http:// at the start and the colon and number at the end. If the TV searches the web instead, it went into a search box: look for the address bar.
            4. Try another address. When HomeFlix is on, other addresses to try are listed here.
            5. Allow it through the Mac’s firewall. If macOS asked “Do you want the application to accept incoming network connections?”, the answer needs to be Allow. To fix it later, open System Settings, then Network, then Firewall, then Options, and set the entry for node (HomeFlix runs through it) to allow incoming connections.
            6. Turn off any VPN on this Mac while you watch. A VPN can hide the Mac from the TV.
            7. Still stuck? Switch HomeFlix off and on again, then restart the TV’s browser.

            HomeFlix runs only on this Mac and your home Wi-Fi. Nothing is sent to the internet.
